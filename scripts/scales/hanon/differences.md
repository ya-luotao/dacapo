# Hanon No. 39/40 compared with the common standard fingerings

Source: Hanon, _The Virtuoso Pianist_, G. Schirmer, New York, n.d. [1900], plate 15538, IMSLP #91547
(PDF pp. 51–65 = printed pp. 50–64). See `hanon.json` for the full note-by-note data.

"Standard" below means the widely taught one-octave patterns (ABRSM / RCM-style scale books) as I know
them; I did not check a syllabus book, so the comparison relies on memory. **Confidence** says how sure
I am of the standard, not of Hanon: Hanon's digits come from the scan (see `uncertain.md`).
"Pattern finger" means the finger the scale's own repeating pattern would put on that note.

## 1. Where Hanon's fingering matches the standard

The fingering for everything **between** the first note and the turning notes is the standard pattern
(same thumb positions and the same 3/4 crossings) in all 12 majors and all 24 minor forms. Exceptions in
the middle of a run are only the melodic minors in §3.

- Majors C, G, D, A, E, B, F: identical to the standard in both hands, including the top fingers
  (RH top 5, but 4 in F; LH top 1). Confidence: high.
- Harmonic minors A, E, B, D, G, C, F, B♭: identical to the standard in both hands. Confidence: high.
- Melodic minors A, E, B, D, G, C, F: identical to their harmonic forms both ways, which is the standard.
  Confidence: high.
- B♭ major and D♭ major RH: standard (B♭ 2-1-2-3-1-2-3-4, D♭ 2-3-1-2-3-4-1). Confidence: high.
- E♭ minor LH (2-1-4-3-2-1-3-2) and B♭ minor LH (2-1-3-2-1-4-3-2): standard. Confidence: high.

**The pattern behind these.** Whenever the tonic is a black key, Hanon starts the right hand on 2 with the
thumb on the next white key (E♭, A♭, F♯m, C♯m, G♯m, E♭m), except where the standard already starts on 2
(B♭, D♭, G♭, B♭m). At the top he turns the left hand on 2, between two thumbs, instead of using the pattern's
3 or 4 (all five flat majors, and F♯m, C♯m, G♯m). Before the repeat he chooses a finger that leads back to his
starting 2. None of this changes the fingering in the middle of the run.

C♯ and F♯ melodic descending use the natural-minor fingering, which is the same as the harmonic form's
descending fingering apart from the pitches; that matches the standard.

## 4. Chromatic scale (No. 40)

- "At an octave" (the form dacapo needs): RH 1 on white keys, 3 on black keys, 2 on F and C going up
  (on C and F going down); LH 1 on white, 3 on black, 2 on E and B. This is the standard chromatic
  fingering. Differences are only at the extremes: RH turns at the top on **4-5-4-3** (B6 4, C7 5, B6 4, B♭6 3), LH starts and ends on the bottom C with **4**. Confidence: high.
- The interval forms (minor third, major/minor sixth) and the three contrary-motion forms use the same
  1-3 (2 on the white-white steps) fingering in each hand, starting on different notes, with 4/5 only at
  the extremes. Standard. Confidence: high.
- "Another fingering, which we particularly recommend for legato passages": a 1-2-3-4 fingering
  (RH C1 C♯2 D3 D♯4 E1 F2 F♯3 G1 G♯2 A3 A♯4 B1 …). This is the "modern" or legato chromatic fingering
  family. Hanon's version changes groupings between octaves (second octave RH C2 C♯3 D1 D♯2 E1…), so it
  does not match any single fixed 12-note pattern that I know of. Confidence: low as to which 1-2-3-4
  variant counts as "standard".

## 5. Things that look like engraving slips (reported, not fixed)

- **D major, LH, last note before the repeat (E2)**: a "1" is printed under it. The pattern gives 4 (5 on D2
  follows on the repeat), so E1→D5 makes little sense. The same "1" appears in a second, independent scan of
  the same plate (IMSLP #00875), so it is on the plate, not a scan defect.
- Several digits in No. 40 (e.g. contrary motion from the octave, LH bar 2, first note) are 1s crossed by a
  staff line and look like 4 at low resolution; at 1200 dpi they are 1s, which also fits the pattern. They are
  recorded as 1 (see `uncertain.md`).
